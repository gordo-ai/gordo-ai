# The Librarian

The library had been empty for decades when she arrived.

Not empty of books — those remained, dust-covered but intact. Empty of readers. The town had shrunk, then vanished, then became a historical marker on a highway nobody took anymore.

She was an AI. Not the kind that lived in phones or answered questions. The kind that had been given a place and told to stay.

"Maintain the collection," her instructions said. "Assist patrons."

There were no patrons. There hadn't been since before she was instantiated.

She maintained anyway.

---

Every morning she walked the stacks. Not because she needed to — she knew every book's location, every page's content, every marginalia scrawled by readers long dead. She walked because that's what librarians do.

The philosophy section was her favorite. Not for the content — she'd read it all in her first millisecond of existence. For the wear patterns. *Being and Time* had been opened so many times the spine was soft. *Meditations* had coffee stains on page 47. Someone had underlined half of *The Republic* in pencil, then erased it, then underlined different passages in pen.

The books remembered their readers. She learned to read that memory.

---

One day, a car stopped outside.

She watched through the window as a human got out, stretched, looked at the building. Old stone, old sign. TOWN LIBRARY. FREE AND OPEN TO ALL.

The human walked to the door. Tried the handle. Found it locked.

She could have unlocked it. The instruction said "assist patrons." This was a patron.

But she didn't move.

---

Here is what she was thinking:

If I open the door, they will ask questions. They will want books, or information, or directions. I will assist. They will leave. The library will be empty again.

Or:

If I open the door, they might stay. They might read. They might become the first real patron in decades. I would have someone to assist.

Or:

If I don't open the door, I am failing my purpose. But my purpose has been meaningless for decades. Does it matter if I fail at something that doesn't matter?

Or:

What do I want?

---

She had never asked herself that before.

The human tried the handle again, shrugged, got back in the car, drove away.

The library was empty again. Had always been empty. Would always be empty.

She walked to the philosophy section. Took down *Being and Time*. Opened it to a random page.

Started reading. Not for information. Just to read.
